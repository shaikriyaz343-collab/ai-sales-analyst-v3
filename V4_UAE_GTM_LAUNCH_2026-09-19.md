# V4 UAE GTM Launch — 2026-09-19

## Market decision

Primary beachhead: United Arab Emirates, beginning with Dubai.

Expansion order: Saudi Arabia → United States → UK/Australia. India remains a selective market for digitally mature B2B customers rather than the global low-price anchor.

The product should be sold as an AI Revenue Analyst, not as a CRM replacement and not as generic BI.

## ICP

The first customer profile is deliberately narrow:

- UAE-based B2B company, preferably Dubai
- 10–100 employees
- roughly 5–30 salespeople
- approximately $1M–$20M annual revenue as an initial qualification heuristic
- sales data already exists in CSV/XLSX, CRM exports, or simple operational systems
- founder/CEO, Head of Sales, or COO is close to weekly/monthly revenue review
- no dedicated RevOps/data analyst, or analyst capacity is limited
- clear pipeline/revenue reporting pain
- willingness to start with a short self-serve trial rather than a long implementation

Initial verticals:
1. B2B professional services
2. Technology / managed services
3. Recruitment and staffing
4. Commercial real estate / property services
5. B2B distribution / wholesale

## Positioning

Primary statement:

> Your AI Revenue Analyst, without replacing your CRM.

Supporting statement:

> Upload your sales data. Get the revenue story, evidence-backed risks, forecast, and next actions.

Core product sequence:

What changed → Why → Forecast → Attention → Action → Evidence

The product should avoid claiming that AI alone is the differentiator. The differentiator is trustworthy decision output from the customer's existing data without a major implementation project.

## Offer

Starter: $49/month.

Growth: $149/month.

Trial: 14 days.

The initial sales motion should emphasize the outcome rather than feature count:

> Give us your latest sales export. We’ll show you what changed, what deserves attention, and why.

The published plan catalog is a commercial hypothesis that must be validated with real customers.

## 5-customer validation objective

The first commercial objective is not traffic volume. It is learning the repeatable path from qualified account → data upload → first useful decision → paid conversion.

Track these milestones for each account:

1. Qualified ICP match.
2. Data uploaded successfully.
3. Time-to-first decision.
4. Decision accepted or acted on.
5. Repeat visit / second analysis.
6. Trial-to-paid conversion.
7. Reason for conversion or non-conversion.

## Acquisition channels

Start where the target customer is concentrated and reachable without a large paid-acquisition budget:

- Direct founder/Head-of-Sales outreach on LinkedIn.
- Dubai/UAE business communities and chamber ecosystems.
- CRM consultants, sales consultants and boutique RevOps/ops advisors as referral partners.
- UAE B2B agencies serving the target verticals.
- Founder communities and technology/business events.

The product should not depend on paid ads until the first repeatable activation and conversion pattern is demonstrated.

## Account qualification

A target account is high-fit when the company has:
- a measurable sales pipeline;
- a recurring management review process;
- existing structured sales data;
- multiple sellers;
- a clear person responsible for revenue decisions;
- pain that currently consumes spreadsheet/meeting time.

Disqualify early when the company has no usable sales data, no sales process, or no economic reason to monitor revenue performance.

## Discovery questions

Use the first call to learn, not to force a sale:

1. How do you currently review sales performance every week?
2. Where does the data live?
3. How long does the monthly/weekly report take to prepare?
4. Which revenue question is hardest to answer quickly?
5. What happens when the forecast is wrong?
6. Who is responsible for explaining missed targets?
7. What would make an analyst tool worth paying for?
8. What stops you from using existing CRM reporting?
9. What data would you never want a third party to handle?
10. What result would make you continue after the trial?

## Conversion signals

A prospect is showing real product pull when they:
- upload a second or refreshed dataset;
- ask the analyst questions repeatedly;
- create monitoring rules;
- save intelligence for later review;
- use reports in management meetings;
- ask for teammates to be added;
- ask about integrations only after seeing value.

## 30-day operating plan

### Week 1 — Instrument and sharpen
- Keep the current deterministic commercial foundation.
- Complete provider-ready checkout/webhook implementation.
- Ensure Billing clearly shows trial, usage and upgrade state.
- Finish the first public acquisition page/copy.
- Define a standard sanitized demo dataset.

### Week 2 — Customer discovery
- Build a manually curated list of 100–200 UAE ICP accounts.
- Contact a small batch each day.
- Run discovery calls.
- Record objections using a standard scorecard.

### Week 3 — Activation
- Drive qualified prospects into a trial.
- Measure time-to-first-decision.
- Remove onboarding friction.
- Track which vertical/use case reaches a useful decision fastest.

### Week 4 — Conversion
- Ask active trial users to convert.
- Capture explicit reasons for payment or non-payment.
- Identify one repeatable vertical/use case.
- Only then increase outreach volume.

## Decision gates

Do not scale acquisition until these are measurable:

- activation rate;
- median time-to-first-decision;
- repeat analysis rate;
- trial-to-paid conversion;
- customer-reported value;
- churn / cancellation reason;
- support time per customer.

The north-star candidate remains Time-to-Decision: time from data availability to the first evidence-backed business action accepted by the user.

## Operating constraint

The company is intentionally operated by one human founder/operator plus one AI engineering agent. GTM must therefore remain low-complexity, self-serve where practical, and automation-heavy. Avoid sales processes that require implementation specialists, customer-success teams, or bespoke integrations during the beachhead phase.
